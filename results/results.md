## 3. The method used by visualize-it.github.io/double_pendulum

That page uses the rods model in Hamiltonian form (`rods-site`) and forward Euler with a step of 0.1 in its own units (0.0282 √(l/g) on a 1366 px screen). Same start, 60°/60°, difference 10⁻⁶°:

| Method | Swings until apart | Energy error |
|---|---|---|
| RK4 (accurate) | not apart in 30 | 1e-12 |
| forward Euler, as the site | 8.5 | 9e-01 |

Forward Euler adds energy at every step. From 60°/60° the real pendulum is regular; the site's pendulums gain energy until their arms go over the top, and only then diverge.

## 4. Why 60°/60° cannot go over the top

Released from rest, the second arm can reach the top (θ₂ = 180°) only if the energy allows it. For the rods the condition is 3 cos θ₁ + cos θ₂ < 2; for the point masses, 2 cos θ₁ + cos θ₂ < 1. At 60°/60° the rods sit exactly on the bar (3·½ + ½ = 2) and the masses fall short of it (2·½ + ½ = 1.5 > 1): neither can flip.
